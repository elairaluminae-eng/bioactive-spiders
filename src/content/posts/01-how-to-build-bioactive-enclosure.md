Building a bioactive vivarium for a jumping spider (*Phidippus regius* or *Phidippus audax*) is fundamentally different from designing an enclosure for a tarantula or reptile. As active arboreal hunters with 360-degree high-acuity vision, jumping spiders require vertical complexity, reliable cross-ventilation, and precise microclimates that maintain humidity without encouraging toxic mold blooms.

A balanced bioactive enclosure functions as an engineered micro-ecosystem: living plants oxygenate and regulate moisture, a micro-cleanup crew recycles waste, and specialized drainage prevents water table stagnancy.

## Technical Baseline & Environmental Parameters

Before assembly, balance the environmental thresholds required for temperate-to-tropical jumping spider species:

| Parameter | Recommended Range | Biological Failure Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Daytime Temperature** | 75°F – 82°F (24°C – 28°C) | Below 70°F (21°C) halts metabolism and feeding |
| **Basking Spot (Top Third)** | 82°F – 85°F (28°C – 29.5°C) | Temperatures >90°F (32°C) cause fatal desiccation |
| **Relative Humidity (RH)** | 60% – 75% | Below 50% leads to stuck molts; above 85% causes lung issues |
| **Airflow Architecture** | Dual-sided cross-ventilation | Top-only mesh creates stagnant air and fungal blooms |
| **Lighting Photoperiod** | 12 hours on / 12 hours off (Full Spectrum) | Low light causes lethargy; spiders refuse to hunt |

## Bill of Materials: The Bioactive Toolkit

Do not use standard garden soils, untreated wood, or chemical glues. Chemical residues and unsterilized outdoor items introduce pesticides, mites, and systemic pathogens.

### Core Enclosure & Hardscape
* **Vertical Enclosure:** Top-opening doors destroy spider silk hammocks. Select a **front-opening** or **bottom-invertible** vertical acrylic/glass terrarium (minimum 6" × 6" × 9" for sub-adults and adults).
* **Hardscape Anchor:** Natural virgin Cork Bark flats and thin grapevine/manzanita twigs. Avoid pine, cedar, or aromatic woods containing insecticidal resins.
* **Adhesive:** 100% Aquarium-Safe Silicone (RTV, zero fungicides) or standard hot glue.

### Biological Substrate & Drainage
* **Drainage Layer:** 1 inch of clay hydro-balls (LECA) or crushed pumice (3–5 mm grain).
* **Separation Barrier:** Fine mesh fiberglass window screen or non-woven geotextile fabric.
* **Bioactive Soil Mix (ABG Style):**
  * 2 parts milled coconut coir or organic peat
  * 1 part orchid fir bark (fine grade)
  * 1 part horticultural charcoal (adsorbs organic impurities)
  * 1 part chopped sphagnum moss (long-fiber)

### Living Flora & Cleanup Crew (CUC)
* **Micro-Cleanup Crew:** 1 culture of temperate white springtails (*Folsomia candida*) and 8–10 dwarf white isopods (*Trichorhina tomentosa*). Avoid giant or semi-aggressive isopod species (*Porcellio scaber*), which can attack a vulnerable molting spider.
* **Dwarf Plants:** *Fittonia albivenis* (Nerve plant), *Peperomia prostrata* (String of Turtles), or *Pilea depressa*. All plants must be thoroughly bare-rooted and washed to eliminate pesticide residues.

## Step-by-Step Build Instructions

Follow this assembly sequence. Skipping drying times or mixing layers compromises structural and biological stability.

### Step 1: Enclosure Sterilization & Ventilation Verification
Wash the enclosure using a 5% dilution of white distilled vinegar and warm water; rinse thoroughly and air-dry. 

Inspect your ventilation holes. Jumping spiders have book lungs located on the ventral abdomen; stagnant damp air induces respiratory distress. Ensure there is passive airflow through opposing lateral sides, situated 1 inch above the planned substrate line and 1 inch below the ceiling.

### Step 2: Install the False Bottom (Drainage System)
1. Deposit a clean 1-inch layer of LECA along the bottom of the enclosure.
2. Cut your fiberglass mesh barrier 1/4-inch larger than the interior dimensions.
3. Seat the mesh flat over the LECA, curving the edges up the walls slightly to prevent loose substrate particles from tumbling into the water drainage reservoir.

> **Critical Warning:** Never run a bioactive jumping spider enclosure without a separated drainage layer. Excess misting water pooling directly in organic soil fosters anaerobic bacterial decay and root rot, poisoning the enclosure environment.

### Step 3: Layering the Bioactive Substrate
Add 2 to 2.5 inches of the customized ABG soil mix over the mesh barrier. Pack it down lightly to eliminate large underground air cavities while maintaining enough loft for plant roots and microfauna burrowing. The substrate should be slightly moist to the touch—not dripping wet when compressed.

### Step 4: Secure the Arboreal Hardscape
Jumping spiders spend 95% of their active lives in the upper third of their enclosure. 
* Secure vertical branches and cork bark slanting upwards to the top rear corners.
* Secure pieces using aquarium-safe silicone or hot glue directly to the walls. 
* **Crucial Rule:** If using silicone, allow a complete **48-hour cure time** in a ventilated room until all acetic acid fumes (vinegar smell) have fully dissipated before proceeding.

### Step 5: Planting the Micro-Canopy
1. Wash plant roots in lukewarm water until all nursery potting soil is cleared.
2. Excavate small pockets in your substrate mix and insert the root systems.
3. Firm the soil around the base and cover bare patches with a thin layer of damp long-fiber sphagnum moss and clean leaf litter (crushed oak or magnolia leaves). Leaf litter acts as shelter and food for the cleanup crew.

### Step 6: Seeding the Clean-Up Crew
Gently deposit your springtail culture directly over the moist leaf litter and at the base of the cork bark. Springtails feed on emerging saprophytic molds and fungal spores. Add 8–10 dwarf isopods beneath the leaf mulch to process frass (spider droppings) and discarded prey carcasses.

## The 7-Day Biological "Cycle" Phase

**Do not introduce your spider immediately.** A fresh bioactive enclosure goes through an initial fungal spike 3 to 5 days after moisture, warmth, and organic matter mix.

`Day 1: Enclosure Set Up` ──► `Day 3-4: Fungal Bloom Spike` ──► `Day 5-6: Springtails Consume Mold` ──► `Day 7+: Biologically Stable`

1. Keep the enclosure under normal 12-hour lighting cycles at 75°F–80°F.
2. Lightly mist one wall every 48 hours.
3. Observe the white saprophytic fuzz that appears on cork or leaf litter; the springtail colony will discover and consume this growth within 48 to 72 hours.
4. Once the mold cycle subsides and the plants stabilize without leaf drop, your micro-vivarium is biologically safe for habitation.

## Safe Hydration & Daily Maintenance Protocol

- **The Droplet Rule:** Never place open water bowls or deep bottle caps inside a jumping spider vivarium. Spiders breathe through ventral book lungs; surface tension in a water dish can trap and drown them instantly.
- **Misting Technique:** Using an ultra-fine aerosol mist bottle, spray 2–3 light micro-droplets on the upper side acrylic once every 24–48 hours. The spider will drink directly from these micro-beads.
- **Cleanup Crew Maintenance:** Add a single grain of uncooked brown rice or a pinch of brewer's yeast beneath the leaf litter once every 3 weeks if the spider leaves minimal prey remnants.

## Troubleshooting Common Setup Failures

### 1. Persistent White Mold Overrunning Plants
- **Underlying Cause:** Inadequate lateral cross-ventilation paired with over-saturation.
- **Fix:** Temporarily cease misting for 72 hours. Use an acrylic drill bit to add two additional 2 mm ventilation holes 1 inch above the substrate level on both side walls. Introduce an additional booster culture of springtails.

### 2. Spider Stays Permanently on the Floor
- **Underlying Cause:** Upper zone temperature is too low, lighting is dim, or the spider lacks structural anchors near the ceiling.
- **Fix:** Mount a full-spectrum 5000K–6500K LED puck light 4–6 inches directly above the enclosure ceiling. Verify ambient temperatures reach at least 76°F (24.5°C) at the canopy level.

### 3. Spider Builds Silk Retreat on the Door Seam
- **Underlying Cause:** The top lid is the highest accessible point.
- **Fix:** Use front-opening or bottom-opening enclosures. If the spider webs across an opening seam, carefully separate the silk boundary with a fine paintbrush dipped in warm water before opening to avoid tearing the inner structural retreat.
